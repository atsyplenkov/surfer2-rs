# Issue #1: fast intersections and duplicate ridges

Confirmed on the nine-vertex reduction of basin feature 1, EPSG:32637.

`intersection_velocity` used to treat any intersection faster than `1e6` as missing. The near-parallel pair in that polygon has a finite speed near `1.98e7`. The missing case then stored a zero velocity, the leftover ridge was never traced, and `finite_segments` came back as two coordinate components even though construction returned `Ok`.

Offset subtraction, `p(1) - p(0)`, is the historical velocity. At these coordinates the two intersections sit near `6e6`, and a fast spoke amplifies a few ulps of cancellation into a sub-metre miss at the far end (about `0.82 m` on the nine-vertex ridge when the cap is removed and subtraction is kept). The normal formula, the intersection of the two supporting-line normals with the edge directions, is the same linear velocity and does not subtract the projected frame. It is used only when subtraction is faster than `1e6`, or when the projected frame (`|p0| >= 1e4`) disagrees with it by more than `1e-10` relative. Unit-coordinate fixtures stay on subtraction, including weighted and degree-one cases. Exact parallels still return no intersection.

Using the normal formula for every intersection was tried and rejected. It connected the nine-vertex ridge, then looped a spoke fixture, changed the weighted `random-2` count (`121` to `118`) and the degree-one simple count (`40` to `39`), broke the larger lake DCEL check, and still failed both original basins. A relative cutoff of `0` or `1e-12` does the same kind of damage: vertices whose two formulas differ only in that band change event order and send later vertices outside the basin. `1e-10` is the tightest cutoff that keeps the nine-vertex ridge and those oracles.

The topology change is separate and has to travel with the numerical one. Once a projected collapse resolves, two kinetic records can name one directed pair of DCEL nodes. Emitting both halfedges leaves the second one as an unreachable 2-cycle after the infinity-chord pass (lake 10623, face 11004, halfedge 50738 duplicating 50734). The first directed pair is kept. Coordinates are not snapped, and no oracle expected value was lowered to make that pass.

That pair of changes makes the nine-vertex polygon one tree, with finite positive-length arcs, all nine input corners, and endpoints joined by exact binary64 bits. Debug and the earlier release check agree. Both original basins still fail bounded-face validation. On feature 2 the remaining event is a 3-constraint triangle whose edge times differ by about `1.24e-6` s. The earliest edge has already met. The third vertex, speed about `4.05e5`, is still `0.50 m` short. Stopping the whole triangle there freezes that ridge (face 600 has halfedges but no root). Feature 1 fails the same validation on face 13, including a halfedge that runs out to about `(613157, 13048303)`. Splitting only the short edge, rejecting unit-weight collapse times past the input diagonal, and refusing degenerate snaps longer than the footprint were tried. Each one moved the failure. None of them produced a valid connected skeleton for both basins, so they are not in the tree. The direct-normal experiment stays rejected.

## Continuation, still red

Printed halfedges on `1b6ed8e` match that account. Face 13 includes the halfedge out to about `(613157, 13048303)`. Face 600's open ends are about `(463811.705, 6272413.627)` and `(463811.204, 6272413.631)`, `0.502 m` apart, plus a `7.3 mm` stub. The `0.502 m` is one fast vertex (`4.05e5`) stopped `1.24e-6 s` before the other two edges meet. A second family is a birth whose stored origin does not replay the birth point: `position_at(time_start)` misses `pos_start` by centimetres to megametres when the normal velocity is paired with the far intersection origin. A third is a 1-constraint split successor with `|sin|` about `5.6e-6` (classified `Degenerate`, speed about `3.6e5`) whose natural collapse is `Never`, so it runs for hundreds of seconds.

Uncommitted experiments on top of `1b6ed8e` (birth replay pinned when the miss exceeds `1 mm`, a 3-constraint collapse only when the endpoints actually meet, 1-constraint `Degenerate` allowed to collapse now when the natural event is `Never`, and edge-collapse time taken from the planar closest approach when the residual is at most a centimetre) remove face 13 and face 600. Spoke and the river tree stay on their old counts. Feature 1 then passes bounded validation and still has 58 coordinate components (`5643` arcs, `5694` nodes, rank 7). The gaps between those pieces have median length `51.67 m`, which is the dominant input edge, not a rounding gap. Feature 2 still fails validation: face 2268 owns 78 halfedges and does not close. Its open ends near `(437345, 6284236)` and `(437372, 6284235)` are `27 m` apart, and one halfedge is a `30 km` self-loop.

Stopping every fast `Degenerate` constraint-collapse child (speed above `1e3`) splits the river into 4 components. The river's four such children are real ridges: opposite vertex `3.2 m` and `14.7 m` away, `|sin|` `2.9e-5` and `6.9e-5`, opposing directions. The basin's bad children are the same classification with the opposite vertex `92.8 m` away (the other dominant input edge) at `t ≈ 25.8`, which is half of a `51.7 m` corridor. Zeroing only those far children keeps the river and reopens feature 1's face cycle. Widening the same-time spoke repair from about `7 m` to `200 m` also reopens that cycle. Neither is in the committed tree. The velocity rule is unchanged: a finite fast intersection is not made stationary because its speed exceeds `1e6`.
